# Daily Work Log

A structured daily log for capturing what you actually did at work or on a project, so you can open a past week or day and see the trail.

## The Unique Kernel
Not a blank notes page. Six prompts, one after another: what you did, skills learned, obstacles, how you overcame them, resources used, and people you spoke to. Structure makes it easier to write; a dedicated app makes it less boring than a Notion table you have to remember to open.

## Who It's For
Mikey, CS senior and team lead this semester in a project-based program. End-of-semester performance reviews with a faculty coach keep scrambling him: a long semester, lots of tasks, “I know I did it, but what did I do?” Today that’s reconstructing from memory (or a notes dump that never gets filled in). The proof of concept is for him logging *his* work—not for reviewing teammates.

Same shape could later help someone on an internship show a manager what they did. That’s vision, not this build’s user.

## The Core Loop
Open a friendly home screen → tap into today’s log → answer the six prompts one at a time → save. Come back because the semester is long and the review will ask what you did. Later you open a week (and a day in that week) and read the saved answers.

## Inspiration & Identity
Feel: friendly, appealing to tap into—not a spreadsheet you dread. Entry is guided, one prompt at a time, instead of a table. Looking back can still be a simple list/table of saved days. Analogy from onboarding: “MyFitnessPal for professional growth,” but this PoC is the daily log, not the whole product. Spark: a short video about documenting workplace work, mapped onto project-space work as a “stack of proof.” Laptop speech-to-text is welcome as how you type; it is not an in-app feature.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
“I’m always scrambling, I’m always forgetting, like, I know I did it, but what did I do?” He wants a stack of proof for performance reviews and for presenting what he learned, so he doesn’t blank.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Open the app to a friendly screen. Hit something inviting, walk through today’s six prompts, save. Then open a past week (in the vision: any of 52 weeks; in the demo: enough saved days to pick a week and a weekday) and see what was logged that week, including a specific day—e.g. Wednesday of that week. That’s the “oh, I can actually look this up” beat for a one-minute demo. No AI recap required to prove it.

## The POC Boundary
In: home → start today’s log; six guided fields; save; browse past logs by week and by day in a simple list/table. Enough history to demo look-back (not a seeded full year). Local app is enough; deployment is optional.

## Later
Prioritized after this PoC, from Mikey’s ranking:
1. AI weekly summary/report (vision also includes a daily recap and “what you set out vs. what you accomplished,” plus improvement suggestions)
2. In-app speech-to-text
3. Weekly goals broken into daily goals, with an end-of-week check-in
4. GitHub-style contribution heatmap — promoted to the current build at the learner's request after dark mode and discard were accepted. Shows saved log days and opens their reflections; no GitHub integration.

Laptop OS speech-to-text can be used during the PoC without building it.

## Explicitly Cut
- **Reviewing teammates / manager dashboard** — he chose the self-log as the experiment; team-lead reviews of others are a different product.
- **GitHub commits, issues, screenshots as attached evidence** — from the original pitch; the kernel here is structured answers, not an evidence locker.
- **AI skill tagging from work** — also from the pitch; skills are a field you fill in, not something the app infers.
- **Accounts, sharing links with managers, internships as a first audience** — later product, not needed to prove the log + look-back.
- **A full year of preloaded logs** — the vision is 52 weeks of history; the PoC only needs enough days to open a week and a day.
