The complete journey

The scope said “six prompts, save, look back.” Now the flow includes an explicit Done button, smooth acknowledgments, review before saving, and protected replacement of an existing daily log.

01 · Welcome and start

A personal greeting such as “Hi, Michael,” calm visuals, and a gentle invitation into today's log. Short instructions such as “Let's jump into what you did today” introduce the first prompt.

Catch up on a past day: New entries default to today, but you can choose a past date before answering. The selected date stays visible during entry and review, and instructions refer to that day. Monday's entry written on Friday belongs to Monday and its week, not Friday. Drafts retain the selected date. An existing entry for that date is protected by the usual overwrite confirmation.

First use: How the display name is supplied remains open. There is no account system.

  • The home screen greets the user and offers entry into today's log.
  • Brief instructions lead into the first prompt.
  • New entries default to today and allow selecting a past date.
  • The selected date is visible during entry and review and retained by drafts.
  • Monday's work entered on Friday is saved under Monday, with confirmation if Monday already has an entry.

02 · Answer six prompts, one at a time
  1. What you did
  2. Skills learned
  3. Obstacles faced
  4. How you overcame them
  5. Resources used
  6. People you spoke to

Type an answer or use laptop speech-to-text, then click Done. A brief acknowledgment, such as a thumbs-up, accompanies a smooth fade into the next prompt. Typing alone does not advance.

Blank answers: Allowed on all six prompts. “None” is also fine; the app does not invent content for a blank.

  • Topics appear individually in this order.
  • Done advances after a filled or blank answer, with acknowledgment and fade.
  • The sixth Done opens review rather than saving immediately.

Source: scope.md → The Unique Kernel; Inspiration & Identity.

03 · Review, correct, and save

See all six answers together, including blanks. Change any answer before clicking Save. Successful saving makes the entry available in history and shows a warm thank-you with encouragement to return tomorrow.

Persistence: Saved entries must survive closing and reopening the app so look-back serves the scope's purpose.

If saving fails: Keep the entered answers, show a brief error explaining that saving failed, and offer a retry. Do not show success or change an existing saved log.

  • Review shows all six prompt-answer pairs.
  • Corrections appear in the saved entry.
  • The entry appears under its week and day and remains after reopening.
  • The thank-you follows successful saving.
  • A failed save retains answers, shows a brief error and retry, and leaves existing saved work unchanged.

Source: scope.md → The Core Loop; What “Working” Looks Like; Why This Matters to the Learner.

04 · Edit or start over safely

When viewing any saved log, including a past day, open the three-dot menu at the top-right. It offers exactly Edit and Start Over. The proposed Discard option was removed.

Edit

Start from saved answers and change them. Both actions also apply to past days and retain the original date of the selected log.

Start over

Start from blank answers. Keep the existing saved log until its replacement is confirmed and saved.

One entry per day: Saving a replacement asks for overwrite confirmation. Canceling confirmation or leaving before saving preserves the old log.

  • Every saved log has a top-right three-dot menu offering only Edit and Start Over.
  • Starting over does not erase saved work.
  • Replacement requires confirmation at Save.
  • Cancel preserves the original; successful confirmed replacement leaves one daily entry.
  • Editing begins with saved answers.

Source: scope.md → The POC Boundary; clarified in the stage 3 interview.

05 · Return through Profile → Logs → week → day
Profile→Logs→Choose week→Choose saved day

Group saved logs by week. If a week has entries for Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, show those three days. Each week contains at most seven daily entries. Opening a day shows its six answers.

Week 1, Week 2, Week 3: Each week runs Monday through Sunday. Week 1 is the calendar week when you first use the app. Starting on Friday still puts you in Week 1; the following Monday begins Week 2. Numbering advances with calendar weeks, even when a week has no entries. Logs belong to the week of their original log date, not the date they are later edited or saved from a draft.

No history: Show Create a new log as an action into today's entry.

  • Profile includes a Logs item.
  • History groups entries by week and displays only saved days.
  • First use on Friday falls in Week 1; the next Monday falls in Week 2.
  • Numbering follows calendar weeks rather than the number of entries.
  • Opening a day shows its saved answers.
  • The empty-history action starts today's log.

Source: scope.md → The Core Loop; What “Working” Looks Like; The POC Boundary.

Return to unfinished work

Profile→Drafts→Open unfinished entry→Continue

Profile offers Logs for completed entries and Drafts for unfinished entries. Leaving an unfinished entry retains its answers so you can resume later, including after reopening the app. A draft keeps its original log date.

Draft behavior, protection, and success criteria

An unfinished edit or replacement remains a draft while the existing saved log stays intact. Completing a draft follows the usual review, Save, and overwrite-confirmation rules. Successful saving puts the entry in Logs and removes that unfinished version from Drafts.

  • Profile has separate Logs and Drafts items.
  • Unfinished entries remain available after leaving and reopening.
  • Resuming retains entered answers and the original log date.
  • Unfinished edits or replacements do not change saved logs.
  • Successfully saved drafts become completed logs and leave Drafts.

Source: scope.md → The Core Loop; clarified in the stage 3 interview. This adds draft retention and resumption to the original boundary, without a separate draft-management system.

The feeling you're aiming for

A calm arrival

Soothing colors, a personal greeting, gentle instructions, and a welcoming mood after a workday. Green and ocean-like colors were references; baby blue was an example, not a final selection.

A pleasing rhythm

One prompt at a time, an explicit Done button, brief acknowledgment, and soft fades. Review gives room for corrections; a warm thank-you closes a saved session.

Exact colors, typography, wording, and animation timing can be settled during the technical spec. This companion's visual treatment is illustrative.

Decisions you made

  • Personal calm welcome: logging should feel approachable after work.
  • Done button: signals completion for typing or laptop dictation.
  • Acknowledgment and fade: make moving between prompts pleasant.
  • Optional answers: some topics will not apply every day.
  • Review before saving: leave room to correct answers.
  • Notes-like history: Profile → Logs → week → saved day.
  • One daily entry: at most seven entries per week.
  • Catch-up dates: record past work under the date it happened.
  • Week numbering: Monday–Sunday weeks, starting with Week 1 in the calendar week of first use.
  • Edit versus start over: keep answers for editing; begin blank for replacement.
  • Protect saved work: confirm replacement and retain the old log until saving.
  • Empty history: invite the user to create a new log.
  • Profile → Drafts: keep unfinished entries available to resume.
